Warehouse & stock

Receiving, storage, packing, and dispatch — under one traceable record.

CargoMan controls the movement of cargo through the warehouse with order-, lot-, item-, and barcode-level traceability, handling bulk-style quantities and individually traceable items on the same platform.

The operational problem

Stock discrepancies start with disconnected receiving.

When receiving, storage, and dispatch aren't on the same record, quantities drift, and finding out why takes longer than it should.

Without a connected warehouse

  • Receiving quantities are captured separately from the order they fulfil
  • Zone and lot allocation lives in someone's memory or a spreadsheet
  • Damages get noted on paper, disconnected from the stock record
  • Stock balances are reconciled after the fact, not verified as you go

With CargoMan

  • Receiving is captured against the order, client, and product it belongs to
  • Zone and lot allocation is recorded at the point of putaway
  • Damage and exception capture attaches to the transaction, with photos
  • Stock status and balances update as movements happen

How the CargoMan workflow works

From receiving to dispatch, one record throughout.

  1. Receive against the order

    Cargo is received against the booking or order it belongs to, with client and internal barcodes scanned or assigned.

  2. Allocate lot and location

    Items are allocated to a lot and warehouse zone, with quantities, gross weight, and nett weight recorded.

  3. Capture exceptions as they happen

    Damage or discrepancy is logged against the item, with photos and notes, not on a separate form.

  4. Pack and prepare for dispatch

    Packing workflows group items for the outbound movement, validating against what's actually on hand.

  5. Dispatch and record exit

    Dispatch is validated against the order, with a GDN generated and stock balances updated.
